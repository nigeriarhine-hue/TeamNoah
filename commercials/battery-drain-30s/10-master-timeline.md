anything, including the VO.

## The five frames to get right

Everything else can move a frame or two. These cannot.

| Frame | What happens | Why it's load-bearing |
|---|---|---|
| **231** | Sound drops to room tone for the stopwatch | Teaches the audience this film uses silence on purpose, which is what makes frame 510 work |
| **510** | Music out entirely | The one second in which she decides |
| **534** | The click. Cut, music restart and the loudest SFX in the film all land here | She moves first; Noah moves second. The whole product argument in one frame |
| **587** | The fan starts falling away | The payoff the sound design has been building since frame 0 |
| **750** | VO "Noah." resolves · the mark appears · sonic logo note 1 | Three layers landing together |

Act I takes a third of the film because the problem has to be *felt* before the fix means
anything, and because the audience needs to be taught to read a battery percentage as a threat.
If a client asks where the time is, that is the answer — and clip 01 is where to take it from,
never clip 13.
